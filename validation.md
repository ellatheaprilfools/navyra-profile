# Validation methodology and results
 
`navyra-profile`'s tier estimates are validated against synthetic traffic with known ground truth, generated by `synth.py`. Each synthetic prompt is labelled with its true tier at generation time. Cross-bucket (T3) labels are additionally validated against the real embedding model used by `analyse()`, so ground truth and the tool under test share one definition of semantic closeness.
 
## Method
 
Synthetic traffic was generated at five `template_share` values — 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 — at n=2,000 prompts each, seed=0. Each dataset was passed to `analyse()`. Reported tier rates were converted from share-of-non-exact-traffic to share-of-total-traffic before comparison, matching the conversion used in the HTML/PDF report's waterfall chart. Reported rates were then compared against the known true rates.
 
## Results
 
| True templated share | T1 error | T2 error | T3 error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10% | +0.0% | −0.9% | +0.0% |
| 20% | +0.3% | −1.3% | +0.3% |
| 40% | +0.6% | −1.4% | +0.4% |
| 60% | +0.8% | −1.9% | +0.3% |
| 80% | +1.4% | −1.6% | +0.8% |
 
Mean absolute error across the sweep (single seed, seed=0): T1 0.6 points, T2 1.4 points, T3 0.3 points.
 
**Averaged across 5 seeds**, same shares and n=2,000 per point:
 
| Seed | T1 MAE | T2 MAE | T3 MAE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.62% | 1.38% | 0.31% |
| 1 | 0.73% | 1.62% | 0.25% |
| 2 | 0.24% | 0.37% | 0.39% |
| 3 | 0.54% | 1.37% | 0.38% |
| 4 | 0.31% | 1.03% | 0.41% |
| **Average** | **0.49%** | **1.15%** | **0.35%** |
 
Seed 2 is a clear low outlier on T1 and T2 relative to the other four — shown here rather than smoothed over, since the spread itself is informative: single-seed estimates can plausibly land anywhere from roughly a third to double the average, which is the real justification for reporting an averaged figure rather than any one run. Per-share detail (how error varies with template share) was not recomputed per-seed; the table above reports only the overall MAE per seed.
 
## Findings
 
T1 exact-repeat detection uses text matching, not model judgment. Error grows with true templated share, from 0.0 points at 10% to 1.4 points at 80% — consistent with more templated traffic producing more opportunities for coincidental exact-text collisions between unrelated generated prompts at a fixed n=2,000.
 
T3 cross-bucket detection requires the tool to match semantically similar prompts of different lengths. Error stays within 0.8 points across the full range tested — the tightest of the three tiers at low-to-mid template share, widening somewhat at 80%.
 
T2 same-bucket detection underestimates the true rate throughout the range tested, by 0.9 to 1.9 points, without a strong trend across template share.
 
## Limitations
 
Each per-share point in the table above uses a single seed. Repeat runs at fixed configuration show real variation — confirmed directly via a 5-seed sweep: overall MAE ranged from 0.24% to 0.73% (T1), 0.37% to 1.62% (T2), and 0.25% to 0.41% (T3) across seeds, averaging to 0.49%/1.15%/0.35%. The averaged figures are the more reliable estimate of overall accuracy; the per-share table is retained for its detail (how error varies with template share), not as the most precise single number.
 
Ground truth for T2 and T3 depends on the synthetic generator's paraphrasing. Generation was iteratively refined and validated directly against the real embedding model rather than assumed correct. The generator's vocabulary is narrower than real-world traffic phrasing. These results are a lower bound on real-world accuracy, not an exact prediction of it.
 
T4 near-duplicate detection is excluded from this sweep. T4 is reported as a count, not a rate, throughout the tool, matching how it is presented in the HTML/PDF report.
 
## Reproducing these results
 
```bash
navyra-profile validate --n 2000 --shares 0.1,0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8
```
 
This regenerates synthetic traffic and re-runs the sweep against the installed build. Results will vary slightly from the table above due to the single-seed limitation noted above; the overall pattern (T1 error growing with template share, T2 undershooting throughout, T3 staying comparatively tight) should hold.
 
Note: these numbers reflect `synth.py`'s T3 cross-bucket filler selection picking one random whole clause per attempt (not concatenating multiple clauses, and not deterministically always the same clause) — both properties were found to matter during development and are covered by `tests/test_synth.py`.
